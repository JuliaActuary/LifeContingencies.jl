Valuation and reserves

The valuation date and the information known at that date are separate inputs. Moving a value to year five does not itself establish that the insured is alive at year five.

Three different quantities

OperationIncluded paymentsInformation about status
present_value(ins)All scheduled payments, including issueOriginal statuses at issue
present_value(ins, s)Outstanding payments at the selected phase, discounted to sOriginal statuses at issue; no new conditioning
reserve_premium_net(lc, s)Future death benefits less premiums, including the premium at sContract in force at s, or an explicit state

The lc convenience method is for whole-life insurance with annual net premiums. For term, limited-pay, endowment and annuity contracts, use PricedPolicy below. These are textbook net premium reserves on the issue basis; this function does not implement statutory CRVM or GAAP LDTI reserve methods. Custom multi-state contracts use the multi-state engine.

Issue-date PV and future PV

Let $\bar c_t = \mathbb{E}_{\pi_0}[C_t]$ be the expected payment at date $t$ under the original issue distribution, and $v(t)$ the issue-date discount factor. The interval factor is $v(s,t)=v(t)/v(s)$. Then

\[\operatorname{PV}_0 = \sum_t v(t)\bar c_t, \qquad \operatorname{Tail}_s = \sum_{t>s}v(s,t)\bar c_t.\]

The latter is the strict-future value, present_value(ins, s; phase=:after). The default phase=:before also includes annuity-due installments at s. Expected cash flows remain unconditional unless state is supplied.

using LifeContingencies, MortalityTables

life = SingleLife(UltimateMortality([0.5, 0.5, 1.0]; start_age=60))
lc = LifeContingency(life, 0.0)
ins = Insurance(lc)
@assert cashflows(ins) == [0.5, 0.25, 0.25]
@assert present_value(ins) == 1.0
@assert present_value(ins, 1) == 0.5
(issue_pv = present_value(ins), year_one_tail = present_value(ins, 1))
(issue_pv = 1.0, year_one_tail = 0.5)

Use present_value(ins, 1; state=:alive) for the known-alive value directly.

Only half the original lives reach year one. For this death benefit, at a year-end valuation date, dividing the tail by survival gives the value conditional on being alive then:

conditional_benefit = present_value(ins, 1) / survival(life, 1)
@assert conditional_benefit == 1.0
conditional_benefit
1.0

This division is justified when every remaining payment requires the contingency to be in force at s. A guaranteed annuity may pay after death; its unconditional tail cannot in general be converted to a conditional value by dividing by survival.

The cutoff also matters at issue: a due annuity has a payment at time zero, so its one-argument PV equals present_value(annuity, 0); phase=:after excludes the initial payment.

annuity = AnnuityDue(lc)
@assert present_value(annuity) == present_value(annuity, 0)
@assert present_value(annuity) - present_value(annuity, 0; phase=:after) == 1.0
(including_issue_payment = present_value(annuity), after_issue_payment = present_value(annuity, 0; phase=:after))
(including_issue_payment = 1.75, after_issue_payment = 0.75)

Net premiums

For a unit benefit and annual premiums payable in advance while in force, equivalence gives

\[P = \frac{A}{\ddot a}.\]

premium_net(lc) prices whole life; premium_net(lc, n) prices an n-year term with premiums for that term. Multiply by the sum insured to scale the premium.

premium = premium_net(lc)
term_premium = premium_net(lc, 2)
@assert premium * present_value(AnnuityDue(lc)) ≈ present_value(Insurance(lc))
(whole_life_premium = premium, two_year_term_premium = term_premium,
 annual_premium_for_100_000 = 100_000 * premium)
(whole_life_premium = 0.5714285714285714, two_year_term_premium = 0.5, annual_premium_for_100_000 = 57142.85714285714)

These are net premiums: benefit and premium cash flows only, with no expenses, commission or profit margin.

Policy values and anniversary timing

reserve_premium_net(lc, s) establishes the equivalence premium on the supplied basis and values the net future stream prospectively. The time s is a nonnegative integer policy duration.

At that anniversary:

  1. Death benefits for the year ending at s have been paid.
  2. The current state is observed or conditioned on.
  3. The premium due at s is still part of the value.

For a known alive single life, let $T$ be the future lifetime measured from issue, so $T>s$ means the life is alive at duration $s$. The reserve is

\[V^-_s = \sum_{k\ge1} v(s,s+k)\,\Pr(T\in(s+k-1,s+k]\mid T>s) - P\sum_{k\ge0} v(s,s+k)\,\Pr(T>s+k\mid T>s).\]

The sums end with the contract. This is the value before the premium due at $s$, written $V^-_s$. Once that premium is received it no longer offsets future benefits, so $V^+_s=V^-_s+P$. The engine reports both phases as before and after.

reserve = reserve_premium_net(lc, 1)
@assert reserve ≈ reserve_premium_net(lc, 1; state=:alive)
# At zero interest, one unit will be paid on death. Premiums remain at times 1 and 2.
@assert reserve ≈ 1 - premium * (1 + 0.5)
(reserve_before_premium = reserve, reserve_after_premium = reserve + premium)
(reserve_before_premium = 0.1428571428571429, reserve_after_premium = 0.7142857142857143)

The implementation values that small remaining stream directly, avoiding cancellation between two almost equal lifetime values. A reserve conditional on being in force is undefined after the contingency has become impossible; the default conditioning then throws a DomainError while scheduled obligations remain. Pass a range such as reserve_premium_net(lc, 0:75) for a reserve column: each separate call establishes the equivalence premium anew.

For a joint life, aggregate in-force conditioning and known individual statuses give different policy values. See Joint lives.

Curve origin and selection basis

At every valuation date the discount factor is $v(s,t)=v(t)/v(s)$. Keep the curve's original origin and the life's original select basis. Using discount(curve, t-s) or selecting a new mortality row at attained age changes the assumptions. The curve example demonstrates the difference numerically.

Product valuation dates within 1e-9 / frequency years of a scheduled payment snap to that date (or within four floating-point ulps, if larger), capped below half a payment period. Insurance and endowments use annual frequency. This keeps dates built by repeated additions such as t += 1/12 consistent with the schedule, for premiums and benefits in both phases. Dates outside this tolerance retain their actual settlement position.

Establish the policy premium once

A PricedPolicy keeps the benefit, premium annuity and established premium together. Both products share their issue basis. Premium annuities are annualized: monthly premiums are premium/12 per installment. Supply premium=... when loading an existing policy.

term_policy = PricedPolicy(Insurance(lc, 2), AnnuityDue(lc, 2))
@assert term_policy.premium ≈ premium_net(lc, 2)
term_reserves = policy_value(term_policy, 0:3)
@assert abs(first(term_reserves)) < 1e-14
@assert term_reserves[3:4] == [0, 0]
(premium = term_policy.premium, reserves = term_reserves)
(premium = 0.5, reserves = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0])

Use different benefit and premium terms for limited-pay business. This short hand-checkable example is two-pay whole life. On a complete real mortality basis, AnnuityDue(lc, 20) expresses 20-pay whole life without using the experimental API.

limited_pay = PricedPolicy(Insurance(lc), AnnuityDue(lc, 2))
policy_value(limited_pay, 0:3)

An explicit endowment maturity also works when the mortality table ends below a rate of one:

short_basis = LifeContingency(SingleLife(UltimateMortality([0.1, 0.2]; start_age=60)), 0.05)
endowment_policy = PricedPolicy(Endowment(short_basis, 2), AnnuityDue(short_basis, 2))
@assert abs(policy_value(endowment_policy, 0)) < 1e-14
@assert policy_value(endowment_policy, 2) == 0
policy_value(endowment_policy, 0:3)

Revalue without repricing

A complete basis=LifeContingency(...) replaces valuation mortality and interest. It retains the original age origins, starting statuses, joint contingency and life order. Contract terms and payment rules remain fixed; lifetime horizons follow the new mortality. The stored premium remains the premium established on the issue basis.

new_basis = LifeContingency(life, 0.03)
stored_premium = term_policy.premium
revalued = policy_value(term_policy, 0:2; basis=new_basis)
@assert term_policy.premium == stored_premium
(issue_basis = term_reserves[1:3], revised_basis = revalued)
(issue_basis = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0], revised_basis = [-0.02163257611461966, -0.014563106796116498, 0.0])

A column call uses the established premium with one forward occupancy pass and one backward valuation pass. Single and joint lives use typed fixed-state buffers and cached transition rows; scalar and column calls share the same calculation. It returns a numeric vector aligned with the supplied integer anniversaries, including unsorted or repeated requests. Use state for a fixed known status across the requested column. Fractional policy values require additional settlement information and are rejected.

Guaranteed annuities and observed death

guaranteed_benefit = AnnuityDue(lc, 4; certain=3)
payout = PricedPolicy(guaranteed_benefit, AnnuityDue(lc, 1))
@assert policy_value(payout, 1; state=:dead) == 2
@assert policy_value(payout, 1; state=:dead, phase=:after) == 1
policy_value(payout, 1:4; state=:dead)
4-element Vector{Float64}:
 2.0
 1.0
 0.0
 0.0

At an annual premium date, phase=:before is the terminal reserve ${}_tV$; phase=:after is the initial reserve ${}_tV+P$ when no other beginning-period payment falls due. End-period death, maturity and immediate-annuity payments at that date have already settled in both phases. Guarantees remain unconditional during deferral.

A guarantee can outlive all possible survivors. Default active-state conditioning is then undefined even though guaranteed payments remain; pass state=:dead (or state=(:dead, :dead) for joint lives) to value the remaining obligations. A joint horizon retains both lives' mortality support even if one life was dead at issue, so it may extend beyond the last possible survivor from the recorded issue statuses. Explicit observed states can differ from those statuses; the horizon does not change with them. Once every scheduled obligation has settled, all state choices return zero.

Established premium versus valuation net premium

policy_value retains policy.premium when basis changes. A supplied premium can be a contractual or gross amount; the value is still benefits less those premiums, with no expenses supplied by these unit products. It is not a statutory net premium reserve. To establish a new equivalence premium on a revised basis, construct a new policy:

repriced = PricedPolicy(Insurance(new_basis, 2), AnnuityDue(new_basis, 2))
@assert abs(policy_value(repriced, 0)) < 1e-14
(retained_premium = term_policy.premium, revised_net_premium = repriced.premium)
(retained_premium = 0.5, revised_net_premium = 0.4854368932038835)

reserve_premium_net(new_basis, t) is the whole-life textbook equivalent: its premium is calculated on new_basis. It has no alternate-basis keyword. Neither operation implements regulatory reserve methodology.

For a single-premium payout annuity, AnnuityDue(lc, 1) deliberately represents one premium at issue. Once that premium is paid, use present_value(annuity, t; state=:alive) for the benefit-only liability, or policy_value for the policy's net stream. Premium annuities require certain=0; benefit annuities may still have guaranteed installments.
