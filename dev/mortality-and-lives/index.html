Mortality and lives

A SingleLife fixes the mortality assumption, original issue age, fractional-age assumption and observed status at issue. Revaluing that life later keeps its original selection basis. It does not select a new mortality-table row at the valuation date.

Select and ultimate tables

using LifeContingencies, MortalityTables

vbt = MortalityTables.table("2001 VBT Residual Standard Select and Ultimate - Male Nonsmoker, ANB")
selected = SingleLife(vbt.select[30])
same_selection = SingleLife(vbt.select; issue_age=30)
ultimate = SingleLife(vbt.ultimate; issue_age=60)
@assert selected.mortality == same_selection.mortality
(select_issue_age = selected.issue_age, ultimate_issue_age = ultimate.issue_age,
 select_ten_year_survival = survival(selected, 10))
(select_issue_age = 30, ultimate_issue_age = 60, select_ten_year_survival = 0.9926243203652193)

Pass a rate vector, a select table's vector of rows, or a supported parametric law. Passing the entire MortalityTable is ambiguous; choose its select or ultimate data first. Rate-vector indices are attained ages, and issue age defaults to the first index. A plain Julia vector starts at index 1, so SingleLife([0.01, 0.02]) describes a life aged 1 at issue. Use UltimateMortality(rates; start_age=x) to index the rates from age x.

Durations, ages and fractional survival

For a life issued at age $x$, survival(life, t) evaluates survival to age $x+t$. The two-time form survival(life, s, t) is conditional survival for a life alive at duration s. Use decrement(life, s, t) from MortalityTables for its complement.

q = UltimateMortality([0.1, 0.2, 1.0]; start_age=60)
l = SingleLife(q)
(survival_to_year_one = survival(l, 1),
 second_year_survival_if_alive = survival(l, 1, 2),
 second_year_death_if_alive = decrement(l, 1, 2))
(survival_to_year_one = 0.9, second_year_survival_if_alive = 0.8, second_year_death_if_alive = 0.19999999999999996)

Fractional times use UniformDeaths() by default. Alternatives are ConstantForce() and Balducci():

[(assumption = string(typeof(a)), half_year_survival = survival(SingleLife(q; fractional_assump=a), 0.5))
 for a in (UniformDeaths(), ConstantForce(), Balducci())]
3-element Vector{@NamedTuple{assumption::String, half_year_survival::Float64}}:
 (assumption = "MortalityTables.UniformDeaths", half_year_survival = 0.95)
 (assumption = "MortalityTables.ConstantForce", half_year_survival = 0.9486832980505138)
 (assumption = "MortalityTables.Balducci", half_year_survival = 0.9473684210526316)

MortalityTables 3 derives conditional fractional survival consistently with the underlying survival function. On supported intervals these probabilities compose:

@assert survival(l, 0, 0.5) * survival(l, 0.5, 1.5) ≈ survival(l, 0, 1.5)
survival(l, 0, 1.5)
0.81

Observed status

alive records what is known about the person at issue, time zero. Here that is age 60. alive=true means the person is alive then; mortality determines whether they survive later. alive=false means they are already dead then, so their probability of being alive at any future time is zero. This flag supplies a starting state, not a new mortality table.

The two forms of survival answer different questions:

CallQuestionUses the recorded alive status?
survival(life, t)Given this person's status at issue, what is the probability they are alive at t?Yes
survival(life, s, t)Under this mortality assumption, what is the probability of reaching t if alive at s?No: it explicitly assumes an alive starting state

The rates q above have first-year mortality of 10%, so an alive person's first-year survival probability is 90%. Compare two lives with the same rates and different starting statuses:

living = SingleLife(q)  # alive=true by default
dead = SingleLife(q; alive=false)
@assert survival(dead, 1) == 0
@assert survival(dead, 0, 1) == survival(living, 0, 1)
(
    from_alive_at_issue = survival(living, 1),
    from_dead_at_issue = survival(dead, 1),
    if_alive_at_start = survival(dead, 0, 1),
)
(from_alive_at_issue = 0.9, from_dead_at_issue = 0.0, if_alive_at_start = 0.9)

The answers are 0.9, 0.0 and 0.9. The last value is a hypothetical query of the mortality assumption. It does not say that this dead person can become alive, and it does not change their recorded status. In particular, it is not obtained by conditioning the dead person's actual projection on an impossible alive event.

Use the one-time form for a person's survival from their recorded starting status. Use the two-time form when you explicitly want mortality conditional on an alive starting state. This distinction describes SingleLife; joint-life two-time survival conditions on the joint contingency, as explained in Joint lives.

Effect on payments

Product valuation starts from the recorded status. A life annuity on dead therefore pays nothing, even though a hypothetical alive-start mortality query can return 0.9. Guaranteed installments remain payable because they do not require the person to be alive:

life_annuity = AnnuityDue(dead, 0.0, 2)
guaranteed_annuity = AnnuityDue(dead, 0.0, 2; certain=1)
@assert cashflows(life_annuity) == [0.0, 0.0]
@assert cashflows(guaranteed_annuity) == [1.0, 0.0]
(life_annuity = cashflows(life_annuity), one_year_guarantee = cashflows(guaranteed_annuity))
(life_annuity = [0.0, 0.0], one_year_guarantee = [1.0, 0.0])

An observed dead status is also useful for a last-survivor contract where one insured has already died. The joint life then starts with one dead person and one living person; its survival depends only on the remaining life:

one_survivor = JointLife(lives=(dead, living), contingency=LastSurvivor())
@assert survival(one_survivor, 1) == survival(living, 1)
survival(one_survivor, 1)
0.9

Mortality support

omega(life) is the duration covered by the mortality assumption. It is not generally an age, and reaching it does not by itself prove extinction.

AssumptionWhat is known at its end?Product consequence
Rate vector reaching a rate of 1The life dies by the end of that rate's yearSurvival beyond it is zero, so lifetime products are defined
Rate vector ending without reaching 1Survival beyond the table is unknownContingent payments past the end raise a DomainError
Parametric lawSupport follows the law's domain, possibly infinite or fractionalGive textbook products an explicit supported term

A vector whose last rate is at age 100 covers the year beginning at 100. For issue age 60, its life horizon is 100 - 60 + 1 = 41. A parametric law instead uses its own terminal age without the extra year.

incomplete = SingleLife(UltimateMortality([0.1, 0.2]; start_age=60))
@assert omega(incomplete) == 2
present_value(Insurance(incomplete, 0.05, 2))
0.25850340136054417

That two-year term makes the contract's end explicit. Whole life on the same rates is undefined:

try
    present_value(Insurance(incomplete, 0.05))
catch err
    @assert err isa DomainError
    Text(sprint(showerror, err))
end
DomainError with 2:
the contract continues past the end of the mortality assumption at duration 2 (attained age(s) 62) while lives remain in force; supply rates that reach extinction (a rate of 1), or give the contract a shorter term or an explicit Endowment maturity

Supply a longer or complete rate vector when a contingent obligation extends further. Certain payments can be valued past mortality support because they do not require survival. In the multi-state engine, a projection horizon and a terminal value must also state what happens to obligations remaining at the horizon.

Parametric mortality

gompertz = SingleLife(MortalityTables.Gompertz(a=0.0002, b=0.13); issue_age=40)
(horizon = omega(gompertz), ten_year_insurance_pv = present_value(Insurance(gompertz, 0.05, 10)))
(horizon = Inf, ten_year_insurance_pv = 0.3962708063312646)

Most laws have infinite support. Some, such as Wittstein, have finite support whose end need not be a whole attained age. Choose terms and payment dates covered by the law; an integer annual death-benefit term must fit its entire last year within that support.

Compare tables

Keep issue ages and the interest assumption fixed when comparing premiums:

tables = [
    MortalityTables.table("1980 CET - Male Nonsmoker, ANB"),
    vbt,
]
